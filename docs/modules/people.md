# People

Own companies, branches, departments, positions, cost centers, people, employments, reporting lines, and effective-dated changes.

Separate global person/account identity from company employee number, contract, and salary. Support administrative onboarding, probation, contract extension, transfers, and offboarding. A cross-company transfer creates a new employment and preserves the old history.

Foreign identity/document expiry and tax residency are separate attributes. Import supports preview, field errors, duplicate detection, and row outcomes. Referenced historical data is archived rather than destroyed.

Screens: company/employee directories, organization structure, employee tabs, import, scheduled changes.

Acceptance: concurrent employee number uniqueness, effective-dated history, company-reference constraints, safe import replay, and historical payroll unchanged.

## Personal profiles

Sensitive profiles have separate `people.profile.read` and `people.profile.manage` grants. Company administrators and HR templates include these for new assignments; existing memberships require an explicit permission update. Directory access alone does not reveal birth dates or nationality. An employee can read their own profile with `people.self.read`, while profile history requires the explicit sensitive-read grant.

`GET/PUT /companies/{companyId}/employees/{employeeId}/profile` reads or changes the shared person profile. Changes require the owning company, an expected profile version, an idempotency key, and a reason. Account linkage and the owning company are immutable through this operation. The profile version is independent of employment revisions. Profile changes, complete immutable history, audit/outbox, and receipts share one transaction. Employment revision numbers and terms remain unchanged. Directories show the current profile; submitted leave snapshots remain immutable. Payroll must snapshot personal data at finalization.

`GET .../profile/history` uses ascending revision cursors with a maximum page size of 200. Migrated initial profiles have no recorded actor; subsequent revisions retain the actual actor and reason. Company access to a shared person permits reads only when the company has that person's employment and the actor has the required grant. The owner company remains responsible for edits.

Profile reads, history, and edits hold people, company, membership, and account guards and recheck the original/live permission intersection and current MFA assurance before acquisition or receipt replay. A proof that expires while waiting requires renewal, even when a matching committed receipt exists. Organization-unit reads/edits similarly protect the structure before validating live company access. Company creation revalidates platform credentials and `companies.create`; company settings revalidate current company authority. A previously committed receipt does not bypass current authorization.

Employee creation, detail/directory/history reads, employment revisions, and scheduled-revision cancellation also check current MFA after their ordered resource/access guards. An expired request must renew its proof before reading data, changing employment, or replaying a receipt. The original permissions remain an upper bound on renewed live access.

`GET /companies/{companyId}/employees/{employeeId}/employment?asOf=YYYY-MM-DD` provides company-wide employment details for `people.read`. It returns the effective employee and current labels/availability for its four organization assignments and manager in one guarded transaction. People and structure guards precede company, membership, and account guards; current permissions and MFA are checked after waiting. Reference acquisition is limited to those assigned IDs in the same company. Missing references remain null, archived units remain identifiable, and manager summaries omit private profile/account data. The employment version is the current aggregate version while `appliedRevision` follows the selected date. Current reference labels are not historical organization snapshots. Team/self readers retain the existing scoped employee endpoint and cannot use this richer company-wide projection.

Employment creation, revisions, cancellation, directory/detail reads, and history/transfer reads use the same live-access boundary. Directory visibility is recomputed from the remaining permissions after waiting; losing company-wide read access can narrow a response to the caller's own employment. Creation locks linked accounts in stable ID order and rechecks their active company membership. Reads require an explicit `asOf` date where the effective employment view is requested.

## Scheduled employment changes

Future employment revisions can be cancelled through `POST /companies/{companyId}/employees/{id}/revisions/{revision}/cancel`, with an expected employment version, idempotency key, and reason. Cancellation is an immutable record; the original revision remains in history with its cancellation metadata. Effective reads, leave eligibility, and current reporting access exclude cancelled revisions. Version counters continue increasing even though cancelled revisions are not applied.

Only revisions after today in the owning company's timezone can be cancelled. Initial and already effective revisions require a new explicit correction. Cancellation revalidates the future reporting graph because restoring an earlier manager could create a cycle; an invalid graph rolls back the cancellation. Concurrent changes, audit, outbox, and receipts share the existing company lock and transaction. A committed cancellation can replay its receipt after the former effective date.

`GET /companies/{companyId}/employees/{employeeId}/revisions/{revision}` returns one immutable revision, the current employment version, the owning company's current date, and cancellation availability. It requires `people.read`, acquires the shared people guard before company/member/account guards, and intersects original/live access and MFA after waiting. Management affects cancellation availability but never grants the read. Revision zero, already effective revisions, and cancelled revisions remain readable without a cancellation action. A later POST still independently validates its observed version, date, graph consequences, and current authority. The read does not acquire a private person profile or imply that a browser clock is authoritative.

## Cross-company transfers

`POST /companies/{companyId}/employees/{id}/transfer` creates a new destination employment and ends the source employment in one transaction. It accepts a stable destination employment ID, destination employee number and terms, expected source version, reason, and idempotency key. The original person, profile ownership, account, and employment history are preserved. `GET .../employees/{id}/transfers` returns the incoming/outgoing transfer records visible from that company. Transfer records are immutable.

This operation implements an immediate transfer: the effective/start date must be today in both companies, and the source must have started before today. Future revisions and current or scheduled reporting dependents must be cancelled/reassigned first. The destination cannot have another open or scheduled employment for the same person. A transferred source employment cannot be reopened through ordinary revision; a later employment must have its own identity. Target unit kinds, manager eligibility, employment terms, and employee number uniqueness are validated.

The actor needs `people.manage` and `people.transfer` in both companies, recent authentication, and recent MFA when enabled. Company, membership, and current account access are pinned and rechecked inside the transaction. A linked account must already have an active destination membership. Transfer never copies role grants or activates a global account. If no other current or planned employment remains in the source company, `people.offboard` is additionally required and its membership is deactivated with a frozen grant history. Self-removal and removal of the last active company administrator are rejected. Other company memberships remain unchanged.

Both company employment locks are acquired in stable order. A narrowly scoped secondary-company transaction grants only the required read/write access; unrelated tables and third companies stay inaccessible. Employment changes, transfer metadata, membership changes, both audit/outbox records, and the original response receipt commit together. Cancellation, revoked credentials, version conflicts, or a failed destination write roll everything back. Replaying a committed request still requires current access to both companies. Leave balances, compensation, submitted requests, and historical payroll are not copied or recalculated by a transfer; their modules retain ownership.

## Lifecycle checklists

`/companies/{companyId}/lifecycle` owns company templates, employee cases, assigned tasks, and transition history. Templates have stable codes/kinds and optimistic versions, with up to 64 named tasks and 128 templates per company. Each case freezes its selected template version, task labels, required flags, and due dates. Editing or archiving a template leaves existing cases unchanged. There is at most one open case of each kind per employment.

`GET .../lifecycle/templates/{id}` returns one company-scoped template, including its version and complete bounded task definitions. It requires `people.lifecycle.read`, including for direct links; manage/perform grants alone do not expose templates. Template reads acquire the shared template guard before company/member/account guards and recheck current credentials, scope, and MFA. A foreign-company template ID is not found in the selected company.

`people.lifecycle.read` exposes company cases and history; `people.lifecycle.manage` manages templates, starts/cancels cases, assigns tasks, and completes onboarding. An active assignee with `people.lifecycle.perform` can read only their own task queue and complete/reopen their assigned tasks while the case is open. Assignment changes require an active company member with the relevant permission. All case/task actions require an expected case version, idempotency key, and reason. Removing access or reassigning a task removes the former assignee's authority. Required tasks must be done; optional tasks must be done or explicitly waived by a manager. Task definitions and transition history are immutable. Lists/history are paginated with a maximum page size of 200.

`GET .../lifecycle/assignees` provides a minimal assignment lookup for `people.lifecycle.manage`. It returns only account IDs and display names for active company members with lifecycle perform/manage permission. It accepts a literal, case-insensitive display-name query of up to 120 characters and a bounded UUID cursor page (default 50, maximum 200). It does not search or acquire account email, expose grants, or require identity administration access. Identity owns the minimal membership projection; the lifecycle use case supplies the eligibility permissions, guards current actor access/MFA, and publishes the feature's domain references. Choices are live observations, not reservations: assignment/start commands recheck the selected member before writing.

Lifecycle reads and commands revalidate current company, membership, credentials, and applicable MFA under guards before returning data or a receipt. A proof that expires while the request waits cannot authorize a read, mutation, or replay. Original and live permissions are intersected: a removed management grant leaves only assigned-task actions, and a grant added during a pending request cannot expand that request. New assignees are locked in stable account order and rechecked before writing. Case operations acquire people and case guards before access guards; offboarding additionally checks recent authentication after acquiring the case. Case lists fetch each version and its bounded task collection in one SQL snapshot, so concurrent task updates cannot combine an old version with newer task state.

Case details/lists and the assigned-task queue include an `employee` reference containing only the employment ID, employee number, and current legal name. This context is read in the same SQL snapshot as the case/tasks, without loading the private profile or expanding directory permissions. A perform-only assignee receives it only for their own pending tasks in open cases; reassignment or completion removes that queue entry. The reference reflects current identity, while the case's template/task definitions remain frozen. Existing response fields are retained for API clients.

Completing onboarding records completion without granting permissions or changing the employment's terms. Cancelling a case removes its pending tasks from active queues and retains its history. Starting a case and transferring its employment share the company employment lock; transfer requires every open lifecycle case to be completed or cancelled first.

`POST .../cases/{id}/complete-offboarding` additionally requires `people.manage`, `people.offboard`, recent authentication/MFA, and the observed employment version. The case target date is the last working date and must be before today in the company timezone. Other open lifecycle cases, scheduled employment revisions, and current/future reporting dependents must be resolved first. The initiating account's credentials and both membership/company access are rechecked under locks. Self-offboarding and removal of the last active company administrator are rejected.

Completion writes an ended employment revision effective today, retaining the recorded last working date and all earlier effective history. A late completion does not backdate the effective revision or rewrite closed attendance/payroll snapshots. If another current or planned employment remains, company membership stays active; otherwise membership is deactivated with its original grant sources preserved. The global account and memberships in other companies stay unchanged. Checklist completion, employment, access, audit/outbox, history, and the receipt commit together. Concurrent task reopening cannot produce a completed case with unresolved work. Reopening an offboarded employment requires a new employment record; ordinary edits cannot reactivate it.

The current implementation provides explicit HR completion. Scheduling a case does not schedule automatic termination or access revocation; the employee remains active until the completion command succeeds. Scheduled worker execution is tracked separately.

## Employee imports

`/companies/{companyId}/employee-imports` accepts a UTF-8 CSV in the start request, a stable import ID, file name, reason, and idempotency key. `GET .../template` downloads its column headers. Required columns are `employee_number`, `legal_name`, `nationality`, `start_date`, and `contract`. Optional columns are `end_date`, `email`, `birth_date`, `manager_id`, `branch_id`, `department_id`, `position_id`, and `cost_center_id`. Dates use ISO format and organization/manager references are existing UUIDs. The parser supports RFC 4180 quoting, embedded newlines, Unicode, and a UTF-8 BOM; ambiguous headers and malformed records are rejected.

Each file is limited to 512 KiB, 5,000 logical rows, and 1,024 characters per cell. Wide datasets may need multiple batches. The original CSV is not retained or logged; bounded typed proposals and their field errors remain in company-scoped storage. Every occurrence of a duplicate employee number within the file is identified. Import does not match people by email, create accounts, assign permissions, or link accounts automatically.

Starting an import queues a preview job without creating employees. `GET .../{id}`, `/rows`, and `/attempts` expose progress and bounded result pages. The caller needs `people.import`, `people.manage`, `people.profile.read`, and `people.profile.manage`. Start, apply, resume, and cancel require recent authentication and recent MFA when enabled. Preview validates profiles, dates, contract requirements, employee number conflicts, organization kinds, and manager eligibility. Initial imported employments are active; referenced managers must already exist.

Reads and commands revalidate active company membership, credentials, and every required grant under company/member/account guards. Receipt replay requires the same current authority. Commands check recent authentication after acquiring their resource guards and again after waiting for queue capacity. Resume and cancellation acquire the previous job before the import, matching worker order. Detail and result reads hold a shared import guard so a worker cannot combine a newer row outcome with an older header/count snapshot.

`POST .../{id}/apply` uses the observed import version. Invalid rows block confirmation unless `allowPartial` is explicitly enabled. Applying revalidates current references and employee numbers because data may have changed since preview. Each successful row atomically creates person/profile/employment history, records its outcome and audit, and advances its checkpoint. Known row conflicts become rejected outcomes; a technical transaction failure rolls back the entire row. Other rows can succeed independently. `COMPLETED` means processing finished; callers must inspect applied, rejected, and invalid counts.

Cancellation stops further work without undoing committed rows. After a failed/cancelled or exhausted job, `POST .../{id}/resume` creates an attempt for remaining rows under the current operator's authority. Previous outcomes and identities stay immutable. A cancelled import header is terminal. Workers recheck live account credentials and company grants at every step, fence expired leases, and commit final status with the successful job. Old workers cannot mutate a resumed attempt. Queue capacity, retry attempts, operation deadlines, and shutdown use the shared bounded worker runtime. Account linkage remains an explicit identity-management operation, separate from importing employment data.

## Account binding

Imported or administratively created employees can later receive an initial account binding through `POST /companies/{companyId}/employees/{employeeId}/account-link`. The command requires `accountId`, the expected profile version, a reason, and an idempotency key. The actor needs `people.account.link`, `people.profile.manage`, and `identity.manage`, recent authentication/MFA, and the owning company. New company-administrator assignments include the link grant; existing memberships require explicit assignment. A different authorized operator must perform the binding.

The target must be an active account with an active membership in that company. The command neither grants permissions nor activates accounts, and it never infers identity from email. An account can identify only one person globally. An existing binding cannot be replaced or removed by ordinary profile editing or this command. Employment terms remain unchanged.

Company/membership and account administration locks serialize binding against transfers, offboarding, and account revocation. Live actor access is rechecked before mutation or receipt replay. A concurrent profile change rejects the obsolete version. The immutable binding authorization, person profile version/history, audit/outbox, and receipt commit together. A deferred database constraint rejects a binding without the corresponding profile history. Profile history now includes the account identity valid at each revision; existing revisions are backfilled from the previously immutable account binding. Current self-service access follows the account and company membership after a successful binding.
